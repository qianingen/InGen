# Week 5 Dev Log

This week I applied statistical analysis, time-series methods, and predictive modelling to the validated 10,000-row telemetry dataset. The exploratory analysis covered 15 continuous fields using histograms, Q-Q plots, and D’Agostino’s K² normality tests. I also completed 210 Pearson and Spearman correlation tests, reporting coefficients, p-values, Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted p-values, and sample sizes. IQR and z-score outlier methods were cross-checked against seven sliding-window anomaly flags, treating the existing detector as a reference rather than verified ground truth.

The statistical test that changed a modelling decision was the Augmented Dickey-Fuller test. The original battery SoC series produced an ADF statistic of -2.319326 and p = 0.165811 with 9,961 observations, so the unit-root null was not rejected. I therefore applied first differencing and selected d = 1. After differencing, the ADF test returned a statistic of -26.743806 with p < 0.001 and 9,960 observations, supporting stationarity. The resulting ARIMA(0,1,1) model achieved RMSE 4.4938 and MAE 1.9688 on a chronological 2,000-row test window, outperforming the naive persistence baseline.

For anomaly classification, I constructed five mutually exclusive proxy classes from the Week 3 rule flags while excluding leakage-derived features. A balanced random forest was selected using training-only five-fold cross-validation and achieved held-out macro-F1 0.7510. These labels remain rule-derived rather than independently human-labelled ground truth. The final repository suite passed 136 tests with 98.11% coverage, and black, flake8, and mypy all passed.
